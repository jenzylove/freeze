Proof

Every freeze here is real.

Two freezes made through PayBox on Arbitrum mainnet, with real money, each one approved by the user in PayBox. Every step links to its transaction.

Card 16686: one message, two approvals

Live on mainnet

One message in Claude with PayBox: "hey chat, btc dipped today but payday is in 7 days can you pull up my freeze plugin so i lock price for $20 btc". Claude searched the user's plugins, found Freeze, quoted it and picked 8 days so the freeze runs past payday. Then PayBox asked for two approvals.

  1. 01Allowed freeze fees to be taken from the wallet, up to $5 of USDC in total. See it on Arbiscan
  2. 02Froze 0.00024504 BTC at $81,617.28 for 8 days, until 25 October 2026, for a $0.43 fee. See it on Arbiscan

Open card 16686 to see what it is worth right now, read live from Arbitrum.

Card 16685: a wallet that held only ETH

Live on mainnet

The first freeze from this wallet, made an hour earlier. It held only ETH, so it took three approvals: get USDC for the fee, allow the fee, freeze.

  1. 01Swapped about $1 of ETH into $0.9995 of USDC to pay fees. See it on Arbiscan
  2. 02Allowed up to $0.42 of USDC to be taken for the fee. See it on Arbiscan
  3. 03Froze 0.00024543 BTC at $81,486.80 until 24 October 2026 for a $0.40 fee. See it on Arbiscan

How a card pays out

Tested on a copy of Arbitrum mainnet

Both cards above settle when their owner cashes them before the end date. To test the whole payout before going live, we copied Arbitrum mainnet to a private test chain, bought a freeze and moved the price on that copy.

  1. 01Froze 0.04 ETH at $2,418.67 for 60 days. The fee was 8.09 USDC, exactly the quote.
  2. 02Set the ETH price on the copy to $2,900. The card showed 19.25 USDC, which is ($2,900 minus $2,418.67) times 0.04.
  3. 03A stranger tried to cash the card and was refused. The owner cashed it and received 19.25 USDC.
