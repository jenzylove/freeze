What is proven, and what is not yet.

The market is real

Every Freeze is a call option bought from Hegic's pool on Arbitrum. The fees on the home page are read live from that pool, not invented. Each option is an NFT in the buyer's wallet. Anyone can look one up on the card page, for example card 16684 (ETH) or card 16681 (BTC).

The fork run

Simulated on a local fork of Arbitrum mainnet

On 17 October 2026 we copied Arbitrum mainnet to a local test chain at block 515,564,054 and ran a full freeze there. No real money moved and nothing below happened on the live chain. The test price rise was faked on the fork to see the payout.

  1. 1Asked the pool for a fee: 0.04 ETH for 60 days cost 8.09 USDC.
  2. 2Bought it. The test buyer paid exactly the quote, 8.09 USDC, and got a freeze card (number 16685) in their wallet.
  3. 3The card stored the frozen price: 0.04 ETH at $2,418.67.
  4. 4Faked a price of $2,900 on the fork. The card showed a payout of 19.25 USDC, which is ($2,900 minus $2,418.67) times 0.04.
  5. 5A stranger could not cash the card. The owner could, and received 19.25 USDC.

Card 16685 exists only on that local fork. It is not on Arbitrum mainnet, so the card page will say it is not found.

Real mainnet freeze through PayBox

Coming

A real freeze, bought on Arbitrum mainnet through PayBox with one approval, is not done yet. When it is, its card and the Arbiscan transaction will be linked here. Until then there is nothing to link, and we are not showing anything pretending to be it.

What this does not prove

The fork run proves the pool's rules: the fee, the card, the payout and who can cash it. It does not prove that PayBox can send these transactions on Arbitrum. That is the next step.
